soundpost

sends text through the air as sound. no internet, no bluetooth, just a speaker and a mic.

send

0 chars ctrl+enter plays too

receive

start listening, then hit play (on this or another device)

how it works

every character becomes one short chirp made of two tones playing at the same time. three leader chirps go first so the decoder knows where the message starts (five in 2x mode — the count is how the decoder knows), and an end marker (higher than any letter) says when to stop. one extra chirp rides at the end as a checksum, so the decoder can tell you it misheard something instead of silently handing you garbage. the decoder listens on the mic and matches what it hears against the tone table. 


  
known issues:

  
    
    
  • needs a quiet room, the mic hears absolutely everything
    • 
    
  • repeated letters (ll, ss) sometimes merge into one. the gap between chars is 90ms to make that less likely — 2x mode doesn't have this problem since doubles are on purpose there
    • 
    
  • the checksum catches a bad chirp but can't fix it, it just tells you to send again. 2x mode is the closest thing to an actual fix so far
    • 
    
  • wav decoding only understands files the save wav button wrote (16 bit mono pcm). feed it music and you get garbage back
    • 
    
  • only handles a-z, 0-9 and basic punctuation, everything else gets dropped before sending
    • 
    
  • if nothing decodes, turn your volume up. the level readout below the spectrogram shows what the mic is picking up
